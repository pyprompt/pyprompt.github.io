Privacy Policy

Last updated 2 August 2026

PyPrompt does not collect any data. There are no analytics, no tracking, no advertising, and no crash reporting. There is no account to create, and no server operated by the developer that your device talks to.

Where your data lives

On your device. Python scripts run entirely on your device. What you write and run stays there.

In your iCloud account. Session history and app data sync between your own devices through iCloud, using your private iCloud database. This is your personal iCloud storage — the developer has no access to it and cannot read what it contains.

In your keychain. Anything you choose to save through PyPrompt's keychain access, such as an API key, is stored in the system keychain and syncs through iCloud Keychain. Again, the developer has no access to it.

Between your devices, directly. PyPrompt can connect to other devices running PyPrompt on the same local network, which is why iOS asks for local network permission. That data travels straight from one device to the other and never passes through any server operated by the developer.

Two things worth knowing

Local network connections are not encrypted. Data sent between peers on your local network travels in the clear, and a device on the same network can connect to a session you are hosting. Use this feature on networks you trust.

Scripts you run can send data themselves. Python code you write can send data over connections it opens, such as a peer-to-peer session you have established. Anything that leaves your device that way is directed by you, not by the developer.

Third parties

PyPrompt does not share data with third parties, because it does not collect any. iCloud and iCloud Keychain are provided by Apple and governed by Apple's Privacy Policy.

Changes

If this policy changes, the updated version will be posted on this page with a new date. Significant changes will be noted in the app's release notes.
